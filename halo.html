Halo

Our vertical takeoff, vertical landing rocket.

About Halo

Halo is our largest liquid bi-propellant rocket, designed to be a vertical take off, vertical landing (VTVL) demonstrator. Halo is the culmination of our propulsion and avionics design skill and is a representation of our best work.

Leadership

Halo is split into two teams, each run by a chief engineer. Click anyone for their contact info.

Specifications

Type
VTVL demonstrator

Takes off, hovers, and lands on its own engine.

  1. 01

    A 30 m hop, not a high-altitude flight

    The goal is controlled vertical flight and a powered landing, so the vehicle only needs to reach about 30 m.

  2. 02

    Reliability over raw performance

    A hop doesn’t need launch-vehicle efficiency. It needs an engine that behaves the same way every time across a wide throttle range.

  3. 03

    Sized as a 100 kg class vehicle

    Thrust, tank size, and propellant load all follow from keeping a roughly 100 kg vehicle in the air for about 40 seconds.

Thrust
2.5 kN

Raised from 1.5 kN to give the landing more authority.

  1. 01

    Raised from 1.5 kN to 2.5 kN

    Initial sizing gave 1.5× thrust-to-weight. Trajectory and control analysis showed the descent needs more margin to arrest sink rate and correct for gusts.

  2. 02

    Headroom up to 3.0 kN

    The feed system is sized for 120% thrust so the controller has room above the hover point.

  3. 03

    Nozzle built for sea level

    An expansion ratio of 3.65 brings the exhaust out at about 0.95 bar, close to ambient, so the nozzle is efficient where Halo actually flies.

Propellants
N2O / IPA

Nitrous oxide and isopropyl alcohol.

  1. 01

    Nitrous oxide as oxidizer

    Non-hypergolic, low toxicity, inexpensive, and storable at high pressure without cryogenics, which keeps handling practical for a student team.

  2. 02

    Isopropyl alcohol as fuel

    In small engines IPA burns 2–5% more efficiently than ethanol, offsetting most of ethanol’s theoretical edge, and it meters more consistently through small orifices.

  3. 03

    Mixture ratio 2.1 at 330 psia

    Chosen with NASA CEA to balance efficiency against chamber heating: about 270 s theoretical vacuum specific impulse at 1957 K.

Considered and ruled out

  • Liquid oxygencryogenic handling and boil-off
  • Ethanollower combustion efficiency at small scale
Oxidizer state
0 °C · 1000 psia

Chilled and supercharged so it stays a pure liquid.

  1. 01

    Supercharged well above saturation

    At 0 °C nitrous sits at 523 psia. Pressurizing to 1000 psia adds 477 psi of margin, so it flows as a single-phase liquid and only flashes at the injector.

  2. 02

    Regulated nitrogen pressurant

    Nitrogen stored at 4500 psia is regulated down to hold the tank at 1000 psia, so thrust depends on throttle position alone and doesn’t fade as the tank empties.

  3. 03

    Colder means denser

    Nitrous is about 9% denser at 0 °C (930 vs. 852 kg/m³), stretching the burn from 40 s to 43.4 s from the same tank.

  4. 04

    Insulated to hold temperature

    One inch of insulation and white paint keep warming under 5 °C on a 100 °F desert day.

Considered and ruled out

  • Self-pressurized blowdowntwo-phase flow, instability, and thrust that fades as the tank empties
Throttle
Dual VACV

A variable-area cavitating venturi on each propellant line.

  1. 01

    Isolated from the chamber

    While the venturi is cavitating, flow is set by upstream pressure and pintle stroke, not chamber pressure, so combustion oscillations can’t feed back into the plumbing.

  2. 02

    Nearly linear response

    With a shallow pintle angle, mass flow tracks stroke almost linearly, giving the controller a simple, repeatable command-to-flow law.

  3. 03

    One valve per propellant

    Separate valves on the oxidizer and fuel lines allow independent mixture-ratio control, so the team can correct for anything off-nominal found in testing.

Considered and ruled out

  • Servo ball or butterfly valvesnonlinear, with stiction and hysteresis
  • Needle or globe valvessensitive to debris and flashing
  • Feed-pressure throttlingcouples into mixture ratio
  • PWM solenoidscan excite feed and chamber acoustics
  • Variable-area pintle injectorlocks the mixture ratio
Injector
Pintle

A stable spray across the whole throttle range.

  1. 01

    Built for throttling

    A pintle’s spray stays stable across a wide range of flow rates and doesn’t rely on precisely aligned impinging jets.

  2. 02

    Nitrous inside, fuel outside

    N2O exits radially through 32 holes of 1.0 mm in a 14 mm pintle into a sheet of IPA. That puts the difficult two-phase flow where published test data applies.

  3. 03

    Three variants, then choose

    Three pintle geometries will be cold-flowed with real nitrous, and the one that best hits the target flow rates moves on to hot fire.

Considered and ruled out

  • Doublet or triplet impinging injectorsneed precise jet alignment
Engine
Copper → regen

A heat-sink engine for testing, a regen engine for flight.

  1. 01

    Start with a copper heat-sink engine

    Copper pulls heat away from the throat quickly, and its inserts can be swapped between tests. It proves out the feed system and models before committing to expensive hardware.

  2. 02

    Regeneratively cooled for flight

    A 40-second burn is too long for a heat-sink engine, so the flight engine runs propellant through cooling channels in the chamber wall.

  3. 03

    3D-printed Inconel 718

    Walls are expected near 800 °C, where creep begins. Inconel 718 resists it, additive manufacturing builds the channels into the wall, and spare IPA is carried for film cooling.

  4. 04

    Compact chamber

    78 mm chamber, 31.8 mm throat, and 288 mm long overall, with a characteristic length of 1.0 m.

Considered and ruled out

  • Heat-sink engine for flightcan’t survive a 40 s burn
Ignition
Torch igniter

A gaseous hydrogen–oxygen flame lights the engine.

  1. 01

    A sustained flame, not a one-shot charge

    The torch keeps burning through startup, so a timing miss doesn’t scrub the run. It’s reusable and can be tuned between tests.

  2. 02

    Gaseous hydrogen and oxygen

    Both are already gases, so the igniter avoids conditioning cold liquid nitrous and atomizing alcohol inside a tiny chamber.

Considered and ruled out

  • APCP pucksunreliable and made in-house
  • Black-powder cartridgesburn about 1 s, so timing is critical
Status
Preliminary design

Design review done, hardware testing next.

  1. 01

    Preliminary Design Review

    The fluid system design is documented in the team’s PDR, v1.1, October 2026.

  2. 02

    Next: bench-test the valves

    Valves are validated on the bench, then a regulator test station and the remote pressure regulator are built and tested.

  3. 03

    Then tanks, cold flow, and hot fire

    The IPA and N2O tanks are tested, nitrous ground support equipment is prepared, the injector is cold-flowed, and the copper engine is hot-fired.
